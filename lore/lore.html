THE HISTORY OF MEATWORLD

Welcome to the end of the food chain. Two centuries ago, the Earth as we knew it ceased to exist. The biosphere was forced to transition from a world driven by photosynthesis and clean water to an ecosystem fueled entirely by decay, carrion, and marrow[cite: 4]. This archive documents the loss of the Old Gods and the violent chemical reality of our new planet.

THE RAPTURE OF ROT

When the MEAT first consumed the earth 200 years ago, survivors viewed it through the lens of Revelation—a literal Hell on Earth where the unworthy were left behind[cite: 5]. The oceans choked with fat and blood runoff, and traditional aquatic life dissolved[cite: 4]. In the decades following the ground zero event, the concept of a loving, omnipotent creator was largely abandoned[cite: 5]. The few ancient texts that survived were grossly misunderstood; theological concepts like "the Word became Flesh" were taken literally, viewed not as a miracle, but as the prophetic curse that doomed humanity[cite: 5].

THE CHEMICAL ATMOSPHERE

With a global layer of rotting biomatter, the atmosphere is a volatile, toxic hazard[cite: 4]. It is a deadly chemical cocktail produced by the breakdown of proteins and fats[cite: 4].

  • Methane: Highly flammable. Traps heat, creating a severe global greenhouse effect[cite: 4].
  • Hydrogen Sulfide: Lethally toxic and heavier than air, pooling in low areas like trenches and the Deepgut[cite: 4].
  • Cadaverine & Putrescine: General cellular decay that produces the signature, overwhelming stench of the world[cite: 4].

TRIBES OF THE FLESH

Humanity is fractured. Survival in the Meatworld requires entirely new belief systems built around navigating a digestive ecosystem[cite: 5]. From the religious zealots in the bone-wastes to the quiet naturalists of the swamps, these are the dominant factions fighting for dominance in the rot.

THE BONEDUSTERS

Religious zealots living in the colossal ribcages of The Maw. They represent the terrifying extreme of religious adaptation[cite: 5]. Instead of fighting the hostile environment, they have embraced it as divine, grinding bone into paint and worshipping the central MEAT brain as divinity incarnate[cite: 5]. To be digested is to reach "Salvation"[cite: 5].

THE CRUSTLANDERS

The innovators and pioneers of the sun-baked crust who skin the earth for Terrahide leather[cite: 5, 7]. They follow the Doctrine of the Spark, blending humanism and industrial worship[cite: 5]. To a Crustlander, the MEAT is not a god or a demon; it is simply unrefined fuel provided by the universe for them to conquer[cite: 5].

THE DEEPGUT DELVERS

Explorers and spelunkers of the highest order, venturing into the darkest tracts of the inner meat[cite: 5]. Their religion is tied to discovery; they believe that somewhere deep within the world's biology lies "The Core" or "The Seed"[cite: 5]. A Deepgut's journal is their prayer book, and they treat cartography as a spiritual duty[cite: 5].

THE SCABKINS

Nomadic warmongers living a brutal existence on the scarred plains[cite: 5]. They view the apocalypse as a Great Test to burn away the weak[cite: 5]. God is the "Great Adversary," and he doesn't want prayers—he wants to see you fight[cite: 5]. They venerate scars as holy scriptures written on the body[cite: 5].

THE SEEP FOLK

The heartbeat of Meatworld. They live in symbiotic harmony with the living bogs, practicing quiet communion and listening to the literal pulse of the planet rather than fighting it[cite: 5]. They navigate the acidic, liquid-rot environments and neutralize toxic juices to survive[cite: 7].

FAUNA OF THE ROT

One hundred years of global flesh-rot has completely rewritten Earth's evolutionary tree[cite: 4]. Pure herbivores and clean-water aquatics starved out immediately, replaced by hyper-scavengers resistant to necrosis and airborne pathogens[cite: 4]. This archive catalogues the deadliest mutated creatures surviving in the wastes.

CARRION HOUNDS

Feral descendants of wild dogs and wolves. They have lost most of their hair, suffering from permanent mange, but boast incredibly high bite-forces to crush through bone[cite: 4]. They are pack hunters that relentlessly track the scent of fresh blood across the General Rot[cite: 4].

GORE-GATORS

Descendants of crocodilians that lost their scales in favor of thick, leathery, chemical-resistant hides[cite: 4]. They ambush prey from beneath the surface of the Seep Swamps, entirely blind but hunting via vibrations in the sludge[cite: 4].

JERKY-RATS

Hairless rodents with iron-dense, constantly growing incisors capable of chewing through sun-baked muscle[cite: 4]. They travel in massive swarms and extract all their necessary hydration directly from the blood of whatever they kill[cite: 4].

SCAB-SCORPIONS

Massive, heavily armored arachnids that burrow through dried flesh in the Crustlands[cite: 4]. Their stingers inject a digestive enzyme that liquifies the tough crust—or their prey—so they can drink it[cite: 4].

GOLIATH TAPEWORMS

What used to be microscopic parasites have thrived in the Deepgut, growing into massive, blind, segmented horrors that tunnel through the organ walls[cite: 4].

SLANG & TERMINOLOGY

Language evolves based on the environment[cite: 7]. Over 200 years of dust, isolation, and illiteracy, scientific words got chewed up[cite: 7]. Vowels flattened, hard consonants took over, and meanings shifted from literal objects to concepts describing rot, salt, and survival[cite: 7]. This glossary breaks down the vocabulary of the wasteland.

CAPILAR

Derived from "capillary" and "pillar"[cite: 7]. The boots-on-the-ground lawman who handles the exhausting, day-to-day distribution of authority in the Crustlands[cite: 7].

DERMA-SHALE

The geological term for Crustland ground. Derma-shale is made of thousands of layers of dried, peeling, sun-baked skin that crunches and flakes underfoot[cite: 7].

"CHEW THE GRISTLE"

The Meatworld idiom replacing "bite the bullet."[cite: 7] It means to endure a tough, painful, or unpleasant situation because you have no other choice[cite: 7].

LIGGACHER

A linguistic mutation of "ligature."[cite: 7] It is the slang term for the heavy, rusted bone-cuffs used to shackle outlaws, or the title given to the lawman acting as the physical restraint[cite: 7].

TERRAHIDE & SKIVE

Terrahide is the official trade term used by merchants for the thick leather shaved directly from the Crustland surface[cite: 7]. Skive is the everyday, blue-collar slang word for finished clothing made from that material[cite: 7].
